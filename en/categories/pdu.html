PDU — Power Distribution Unit

A Power Distribution Unit (PDU) does one thing: receive power from a source and distribute it to multiple loads. That's it. Everything else — protection, monitoring, filtering, redundancy, radiation tolerance — are options manufacturers stack to meet their domain's constraints.

Manufacturer options

The options manufacturers add

A bare PDU distributes current. Each option below addresses a specific constraint. A space manufacturer will enable all six. A standard datacenter rack PDU may integrate only two. That choice of options — and their quality level — differentiates products and sets the price.

⚡
Option · Overcurrent protection
Passive fuses, thermal-magnetic circuit breakers, or active SSPC (Solid-State Power Controllers). In case of a fault on one load, only that output is cut. Present on virtually all PDUs.
🔀
Option · Switching & sequencing
Relays or MOSFETs allow each output to be switched on/off via logic command. Sequencing prevents inrush current at startup. Added when loads need to be controlled independently.
📊
Option · Monitoring & telemetry
Voltage, current and temperature sensors per output, fed back over a bus (CAN, Modbus, SNMP, 1553, SpaceWire…). Essential whenever the system needs to monitor itself.
🔇
Option · EMI / EMC filtering
Filters conducted and radiated interference to meet standards (MIL-STD-461, CISPR, DO-160). Required in sensitive RF or certified environments.
🔁
Option · Redundancy
Dual input, primary/secondary bus, automatic bypass or cross-strapping. If one path fails, the other takes over without interruption. Reserved for critical applications: space, defense, medical.
🔌
Option · Voltage conversion & adaptation
DC/DC converters (buck, boost, buck-boost) or AC/DC rectifiers to adapt the input voltage to load requirements. Enables multiple buses at different voltages from a single source. Present whenever source and loads operate at different voltages.
🛡️
Option · Environmental hardening
Resistance to vibration, shock, extreme temperatures, humidity, radiation. Conformal coating housing, MIL connectors, space-qualified components. The highest cost driver — each constraint has a price.

Application domains

Same tool, different options

The base PDU doesn't change. It's the domain constraints that dictate which options the manufacturer integrates, at what quality level, and under what certification.

Space — the PDU becomes a PCDU

Satellite · Launcher · Probe
  • All options enabled, at maximum quality level
  • Rad-hard SSPC protection — no arcing, no contamination in orbit
  • Full monitoring over SpaceWire or MIL-STD-1553 — every ampere is counted
  • Hot redundancy — a single failure must never be fatal
  • Radiation hardening (TID, SEE) — components must survive years in orbit
  • Mass and volume minimized — every gram has a launch cost
  • Full ECSS qualification — vibration, thermal-vacuum, radiation testing

Standards required by customers

ECSS-E-ST-20 ECSS-Q-ST-60 MIL-STD-461 MIL-STD-1553 SpaceWire NASA GEVS

Price implications

  • →Rad-hard components: 10× to 100× more expensive than COTS
  • →ECSS qualification: several months of dedicated testing
  • →Typical price: €50,000 – €500,000 depending on power and orbit

Defense & Aero — robustness first

Aircraft · Helicopter · Military vehicle · Drone
  • Priority options: hardening + EMI + redundancy
  • DO-254 certified SSPC or circuit breakers
  • Dual primary/secondary bus — mission continues after partial failure
  • Stringent EMI filtering — no interference with radio navigation
  • Vibration/shock/altitude withstand per MIL-STD-810
  • Monitoring over 1553 or ARINC 429 — standard aero protocols
  • Ruggedized versions for ground vehicles (MIL-STD-1275)

Standards required by customers

MIL-STD-704 MIL-STD-810 MIL-STD-461 DO-160 DO-254 ARINC 429 MIL-STD-1553

Price implications

  • →MIL housing + qualified connectors: significant added cost
  • →DO-254 qualification: mandatory hardware + software audit
  • →Typical price: €5,000 – €80,000 per application

Industry — practicality and integration

Datacenter · Industry · Energy · Telecom
  • Priority options: monitoring + switching
  • Circuit breaker or fuse protection — proven, easy to replace
  • Per-output switching to remotely power equipment on/off
  • SNMP or Modbus monitoring — integration into existing BMS/SCADA
  • Hardening limited to IP rating (IP65/67) for wet or dusty environments
  • Redundancy rare — service continuity ensured by external UPS
  • 19" rack or DIN rail format depending on installation

Standards required by customers

IEC 62368 IEC 60204-1 EN 55032 SNMP v3 Modbus TCP IP65 / IP67

Price implications

  • →Mass-market products — high volumes, controlled costs
  • →CE/UL certification on standard ranges
  • →Typical price: €200 – €5,000 depending on monitoring level

Mobility & EV — high voltage, functional safety

Electric vehicle · Drone · Rail · Autonomous mobility
  • Priority options: HV switching + monitoring + automotive hardening
  • High-voltage contactors (400–800 V DC) with precharge to prevent arcing
  • Galvanic isolation between HV bus and 12/48 V low-voltage network
  • Monitoring over CAN FD — integration with BMS and vehicle ECU
  • Automotive hardening: vibration, salt, humidity, −40/+85 °C cycles
  • Partial redundancy on critical circuits (braking, steering)
  • ISO 26262 (ASIL-B/D) certification for functional safety

Standards required by customers

ISO 26262 IEC 61851 UNECE R100 AEC-Q100 AUTOSAR CAN FD

Price implications

  • →AEC-Q100 components: qualified for vehicle lifetime
  • →ISO 26262 certification: mandatory FMEA + ASIL analysis
  • →Typical price: €500 – €15,000 depending on power and ASIL level
